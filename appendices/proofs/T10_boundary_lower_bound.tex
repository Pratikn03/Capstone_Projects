\paragraph{Theorem T10: Boundary-Indistinguishability Lower Bound.}
Let \(x_0,x_1\) be two latent boundary states with observation laws \(P_0,P_1\).
Assume \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_0,P_1)\le\epsilon\) and
\(C(x_0)\cap C(x_1)=\varnothing\). For any observation-only mandatory-release
controller \(\pi\), deterministic or randomized,
\[
  \sup_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Pr_{O\sim P_i}[\pi(O)\notin C(x_i)]
  \ge \frac{1-\epsilon}{2}.
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
It suffices to prove the deterministic case; randomized controllers follow by
conditioning on the controller's internal random seed and averaging. Define
\[
  S_i=\{o:\pi(o)\in C(x_i)\},\qquad i\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The disjoint-safe-set hypothesis gives \(S_0\cap S_1=\varnothing\). The average
success probability of the release rule is
\[
  \frac12\{P_0(S_0)+P_1(S_1)\}.
\]
Since \(S_1\subseteq S_0^c\), the binary test
\(\delta(o)=0\) for \(o\in S_0\) and \(\delta(o)=1\) otherwise has testing
success
\[
  \frac12\{P_0(S_0)+P_1(S_0^c)\}
  \ge
  \frac12\{P_0(S_0)+P_1(S_1)\}.
\]
The best possible two-hypothesis testing success between \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) is
\((1+d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_0,P_1))/2\). Therefore the release rule's average success
is at most \((1+\epsilon)/2\), so its average loss is at least
\((1-\epsilon)/2\). The larger of the two state-conditioned release risks is at
least that average loss, which proves the bound.
