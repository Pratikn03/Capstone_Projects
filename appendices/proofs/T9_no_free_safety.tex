\paragraph{Theorem T9: Impossibility of Quality-Ignorant Mandatory Release.}
Let \(\Pi_{\mathrm{obs}}\) be total observation-only mandatory-release
controllers \(\pi:O\to A\), with no abstention or fail-closed action in the
policy class. Assume \(C(x)\subseteq A\) is the state-conditioned safe-action
set. For an observation \(o\) with nonempty ambiguity class, define
\[
  B(o)=\{x\in X:h(x)=o\},\qquad
  K(o)=\bigcap_{x\in B(o)} C(x).
\]
If \(B(o)\ne\varnothing\) and \(K(o)=\varnothing\), no controller in
\(\Pi_{\mathrm{obs}}\) can guarantee true-state safety for all \(x\in B(o)\).

\paragraph{Proof.}
A total mandatory observation-only controller must choose one action
\(a=\pi(o)\) for the entire nonempty ambiguity class \(B(o)\). If that action
were safe for every \(x\in B(o)\), then
\[
  a\in\bigcap_{x\in B(o)}C(x)=K(o),
\]
contradicting \(K(o)=\varnothing\). Hence there exists at least one
\(x^\dagger\in B(o)\) with \(a\notin C(x^\dagger)\), so the release cannot be
certified true-state safe on the whole ambiguity class. The claim is restricted
to mandatory release; abstention, fallback, uncertainty expansion, or denial of
release are outside \(\Pi_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
